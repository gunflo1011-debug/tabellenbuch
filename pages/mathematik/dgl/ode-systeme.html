DIFFERENTIAL EQUATIONS · COUPLED DYNAMICS

Systems of ODEs

When several quantities evolve together, a single differential equation is not enough. Systems of ODEs collect the variables into a vector and expose the structure of coupled dynamics through matrices, eigenvalues and phase portraits.

Vector form

A first-order system for \(n\) dependent variables is written compactly as

\[\mathbf{x}'(t)=\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{x}(t)).\]

For a linear constant-coefficient system this becomes

\[\mathbf{x}'(t)=A\mathbf{x}(t),\qquad \mathbf{x}(0)=\mathbf{x}_0.\]

Here \(A\) is an \(n\times n\) matrix. The solution can be written with the matrix exponential as \(\mathbf{x}(t)=e^{tA}\mathbf{x}_0\).

Eigenvalues turn coupling into modes

If \(A\mathbf{v}=\lambda\mathbf{v}\), then \(\mathbf{x}(t)=e^{\lambda t}\mathbf{v}\) is a solution of \(\mathbf{x}'=A\mathbf{x}\). When \(A\) has enough linearly independent eigenvectors, general solutions are linear combinations of these modes.

\[\mathbf{x}(t)=c_1e^{\lambda_1 t}\mathbf{v}_1+\cdots+c_ne^{\lambda_n t}\mathbf{v}_n.\]
Interpretation: the real parts of the eigenvalues control exponential growth or decay; imaginary parts produce oscillation.

Stability and phase portraits

Asymptotically stable

If every eigenvalue of a constant matrix \(A\) has negative real part, all solutions of \(\mathbf{x}'=A\mathbf{x}\) tend to the origin as \(t\to\infty\).

Unstable

If at least one eigenvalue has positive real part, some solutions grow away from the origin.

Saddle

For a real \(2\times2\) system, eigenvalues with opposite signs produce a saddle: one eigendirection contracts while another expands.

Spiral behavior

A complex-conjugate pair creates rotation in the phase plane; the sign of the common real part determines inward or outward spiraling.

A phase portrait plots solution trajectories in state space. It often reveals equilibrium type and long-term behavior without requiring a closed-form solution.

Worked 2×2 example

Consider

\[\mathbf{x}'=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{x}.\]

The characteristic equation is \(\det(A-\lambda I)=(2-\lambda)^2-1=0\), so \(\lambda_1=3\) and \(\lambda_2=1\). Corresponding eigenvectors can be chosen as \((1,1)^T\) and \((1,-1)^T\). Therefore

\[\mathbf{x}(t)=c_1e^{3t}\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}+c_2e^t\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\end{pmatrix}.\]

Both eigenvalues are positive, so every nonzero modal component grows exponentially and the origin is unstable.

Nonlinear systems

For an autonomous nonlinear system \(\mathbf{x}'=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})\), an equilibrium \(\mathbf{x}_*\) satisfies \(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_*)=0\). Near that point, the Jacobian matrix \(J=D\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_*)\) provides the linearized system. When the equilibrium is hyperbolic, the eigenvalues of \(J\) determine the local qualitative behavior.
